# Chance mechanics buy attention, not habits

Casual, idle and habit apps make play compulsive less through chance alone than through **chance stacked on loss**: a random reward on a timer (the golden cookie's 13-second window, a daily spin), a pity counter that turns "almost there" into a reason to keep paying, and a streak that is lost if the player misses the appointment. The lab evidence for the individual pieces is strong in gambling research (variable schedules resist extinction, near-misses and losses disguised as wins raise arousal and urge to play, sound inflates remembered wins by about a quarter), but the evidence that variable rewards outperform fixed ones in games or health apps is thin, and in the one equal-value health trial found, a lottery did no better than a fixed payment while loss framing beat both. The harm evidence is specific to **paid** random rewards: loot-box spending correlates with problem gambling at about r = 0.26 across meta-analyses, stronger in adolescents, with causal direction still open. Legally, in Germany and the EU a random reward that cannot be bought fails the stake element of § 3 GlüStV and sits outside the CPC currency principles, so deshrimp's free Shrimpcoin is not gambling; the residual risks are casino imagery, appointment play and anything that could later become a purchase path. For deshrimp the recommendation is narrow: add a small, disclosed, shuffle-bag surprise on top of payouts it already makes (a daily top-up first, a rare "lucky shrimp" on streak bonuses second, unannounced milestone gifts third), never let chance take anything away, and avoid clickable timed events, coin-bought mystery boxes, near-miss displays, wagers and day streaks. Expect these to make the first days more fun rather than to move long-term retention: nothing in the evidence says chance makes a habit stick, and deshrimp has no analytics to find out.

This report builds on [`idle-game-reward-curves.md`](./idle-game-reward-curves.md), which covers price ladders, prestige and offline progress; it does not repeat that ground except where chance is involved. Since that report, his decisions have changed the numbers: the ladder is now 50, 150, 500, 1,500 and 5,000 (about 4 min, 17 min, 1 h, 3 h and 10 h of straight sitting at 12 coin a minute, 7,200 coin in all), coins are whole, every payout shows in a snackbar, a visited day after the first pays 20, and breaks are not rewarded because the camera cannot tell a break from an empty picture.

## Golden cookies, pity at 90 and god packs at 1 in 2,000: the parameters games actually use

The best-documented chance reward in the genre is Cookie Clicker's golden cookie, and its parameters show how carefully randomness is tamed. A cookie spawns between **300 and 900 seconds** after the last one, with a per-frame chance that ramps as ((t - 300)/600)^5, so the mean wait is about **447 seconds** and a spawn is unlikely early and certain at the cap; it stays on screen **13 seconds** and is then gone. The effect is drawn from a weighted table, about **40% Lucky, 40% Frenzy** (x7 production for 77 s), 8% Building Special, 3% Click Frenzy (x777 per click for 13 s), and a tail of ultra-rare outcomes down to a free sugar lump at about 0.015% and a joke message at about 0.003%. Lucky pays the lesser of 15% of the bank or 15 minutes of production, which turns luck into a reason to hold a reserve rather than a jackpot that breaks the economy ([Cookie Clicker Wiki, Golden Cookie](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Golden_Cookie)). The ^5 ramp is closer to a variable-interval schedule with a hidden floor than to a memoryless process, so players learn when to look back at the screen, and the 13-second lifetime is what makes it a summons: the reward is free, but only to someone watching.

Gacha games publish a low headline rate and keep the real rate in a pity curve. Genshin Impact's 5-star character rate is **0.6%** with a hard guarantee at pull **90**; community analysis of crowd-sourced pull logs found that from pull **74** the rate rises by about 6 percentage points per pull, so nearly every 5-star lands between pulls 74 and 80, a curve HoYoverse has never published ([KQM Theorycrafting Library](https://library.keqingmains.com/evidence/general-mechanics/gacha)). A won 5-star is the featured character only half the time, raised to a consolidated **55%** by "Capturing Radiance" in version 5.0 (August 2024), so the worst case for a specific character is 180 pulls; KQM puts the median at **80 pulls** and the 95th percentile at 158 for one copy, and the median for all seven copies at **654** ([KQM](https://library.keqingmains.com/evidence/general-mechanics/gacha); [esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/genshin-impact/genshin-impact-improves-50-50-weapon-gacha-systems/)). The gap between the advertised 0.6% and the felt rate, and the sting of a "lost 50/50", are design features: pity converts despair near the cap into anticipation.

Card packs work the same way with smaller numbers. Hearthstone guarantees a Legendary within **40 packs** and an Epic within 10, gives one Legendary in the first 10 packs of every set, and has added duplicate protection for all rarities since March 2020 ([Hearthstone Wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Card_pack)). Clash Royale ran a fixed, hidden **240-chest cycle** (180 silver, 52 gold, 4 magical, 4 giant) behind timers and four slots, then removed chests, slots and timers entirely in its Q1 2025 update ([TouchTapPlay](https://www.touchtapplay.com/how-does-the-clash-royale-chest-cycle-work/); [RoyaleAPI](https://royaleapi.com/blog/rip-chests-2025-q1-update)). Brawl Stars' Starr Drops publish a rarity table (Rare 50%, Super Rare 28%, Epic 15%, Mythic 5%, Legendary 2%) but let players tap the drop to "upgrade" it before opening, at odds that are not published: the taps feel like agency over an outcome the game has already decided ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/starr-drop-rates-brawl-stars-explained)). Pokemon TCG Pocket places the rarest slot last and runs a "god pack" of all rares at about **1 in 2,000** packs, an outcome valued for being talked about more than for what it pays ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/pokemon-tcg-pocket/rarity)).

Habit apps borrow both halves. Habitica, whose code is open, rolls a literal drop table on every completed task: the base chance is `min(|value - 21.27|, 37.5)/150 + 0.02`, multiplied by a **beginner's-luck term of up to +80%** that shrinks by 5 points per level and is gone by level 16, by `1 + streak/100`, and capped at a daily maximum of 5 drops plus stat bonuses (doubled for subscribers); food, eggs and potions split 40/30/30 with golden potions under 2% ([Habitica, randomDrop.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HabitRPG/habitica/develop/website/common/script/fns/randomDrop.js)). Duolingo uses almost no random drops and instead prices loss aversion: its Streak Wager, which stakes soft currency on seven more days and pays double, lifted **day-7 retention by 14%**, and the Weekend Amulet made users 4% more likely to return a week later ([Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)); letting users hold two streak freezes raised daily active learners by **0.38%** ([Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)).

| Mechanic | Example | Parameters | What it adds besides chance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Variable-interval event | Cookie Clicker golden cookie | 300-900 s spawn, mean ~447 s, 13 s on screen, 40/40 Lucky/Frenzy | An expiry window that rewards watching |
| Gacha with pity | Genshin character banner | 0.6% base, soft pity from 74, hard pity 90, 55% featured | A visible counter that makes stopping feel like waste |
| Pack pity and duplicate protection | Hearthstone | Legendary within 40 packs, Epic within 10 | Removes worst-case droughts |
| Fixed hidden cycle | Clash Royale (until 2025) | 240 chests, timers, 4 slots | Appointment play through timers |

| Loss-framed commitment | Duolingo | Wager: double back after 7 days; +14% D7 retention | Loss aversion, priced in soft currency |

Two gaps are worth naming. No primary odds were found for the mechanics most often called "slot-like" in casual games, the Candy Crush daily booster wheel and Coin Master's spins, and no sources were gathered on losses disguised as wins in games or on energy timers, so those appear below only through the gambling literature. What the documented cases share is that **chance is rarely used alone**: it is paired with a timer, a counter or a streak that makes not playing feel like losing something.

## The gambling lab finds strong effects, the game and health trials find weak ones

Variable-ratio schedules producing high, steady responding that resists extinction is among the best-replicated findings in behaviour analysis, and gambling is its textbook human case ([Lumen Learning](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/waymaker-psychology/?p=2304)). The neural account most often cited comes from primate work: dopamine neurons fire to unexpected rewards and dip when a predicted one fails to arrive ([Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593)), and a sustained signal between cue and reward peaks at a reward probability of **0.5**, tracking uncertainty rather than value ([Fiorillo, Tobler & Schultz 2003](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1077349)). The robust inference is that an unpredictable reward does not habituate the way a fully predictable one does. The popular inference, that games are "Skinner boxes" tuned to an optimal uncertainty, is an extrapolation from two monkeys and Pavlovian cues to drop rates usually far below 0.5; no controlled study found here isolates the schedule as the cause of compulsive play in humans.

The presentation tricks have better human evidence. In an fMRI study, near-misses on a simplified slot were rated less pleasant than clean misses yet raised the desire to keep playing and activated the striatum and insula, regions that also responded to wins, and the motivational effect appeared only when participants had personal control over the gamble ([Clark et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737/)); in regular gamblers, midbrain responses to near-misses scaled with gambling severity ([Chase & Clark 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2929454)). The same pattern holds outside casinos: among 60 avid Candy Crush players, failing a level by one or two moves raised heart rate, skin conductance and urge to keep playing more than a clear loss ([Larche, Musielak & Dixon 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs10899-016-9633-7)). Losses disguised as wins, multiline slot "wins" smaller than the stake but celebrated with win sounds and graphics, raised skin conductance above ordinary losses and, in novices, to the level of real wins ([Dixon et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1360-0443.2010.03055.x), as described in [J Gambl Stud 2017](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10899-017-9695-1)). With sound on, 96 gamblers overestimated how often they had won by **24%, against 15% with sound off** ([University of Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/news/news/music-gamblers-ears)). Illusion of control, people acting as though chance outcomes respond to choice and involvement ([Langer 1975](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.32.2.311)), is what Starr Drops' tap-to-upgrade exploits. No study found measures these responses to loot-box animations directly, so the bridge to games is structural analogy.

The harm literature is specific to paying. Across 7,422 gamers, loot-box spending was linked to problem-gambling severity at η² = 0.054, while spending on non-random items barely was (η² = **0.004**) ([Zendle & Cairns 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0206767)); among 16 to 18 year-olds the link was η² = **0.120**, more than twice the adult figure ([Zendle, Meyer & Over 2019](https://www.rephrain.ac.uk/paper/adolescents-and-loot-boxes-links-with-problem-gambling-and-motivations-for-purchase-2/)). Meta-analyses put the association at **r = 0.26** over 15 studies ([Garea et al. 2021](https://figshare.utas.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Meta-analysis_of_the_relationship_between_problem_gambling_excessive_gaming_and_loot_box_spending/22996898)) and a mean r = 0.27, with 12 of 13 publications positive ([Spicer et al. 2022](https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/handle/10026.1/17268)). A preregistered study that broke loot boxes into seven features found that paying was what carried the link, with near-miss displays and cash-out adding little ([Zendle et al. 2020](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/148267/)). Causality is contested: six-month longitudinal studies show loot-box use predicting later gambling, but the same work found gambling-related distortions predicting later loot-box spending, and impulsivity is a plausible common cause ([Brooks & Clark 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563222004253); [BMC Psychology 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-025-02766-1)). Critics note that r = 0.26 is about 7% of shared variance ([Chew 2025](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/86434/1/Chew_2025_Reply%20to%20%E2%80%9CFundamental%20flaws%20in%20the%20design%20and%20reporting%20of%20Chew%20and%20Neo%20%282024%29%E2%80%9D.pdf)). The honest reading is that free, earned random rewards have not been shown to carry the same risk, partly because almost nobody has studied them in isolation.

Loss mechanics outperform chance where they have been tested head to head. In an RCT of 281 adults with equal expected value across arms, a daily lottery met the step goal on **36%** of days, a fixed gain on 35%, control on about 30%, and only loss framing (money given up front and taken per missed day) reached a significant **45%**; all arms fell back once payments stopped ([Patel et al. 2016](https://pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2016/february/to-encourage-physical-activity)). Lotteries do work against nothing: lottery and deposit-contract arms each lost about 4 lb more than control in a 16-week weight trial, with partial regain by seven months ([Volpp et al. 2008](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/financialincentweightLoss.pdf)). In games, the earlier report already found that variable over fixed ratio made no significant difference to play time in a small University of Tasmania study ([UTAS](https://eprints.utas.edu.au/47622/)) and that players prefer variable rewards except when the range includes very small or zero payouts ([Kao 2012](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/kao2012rewardpreference.pdf)). Among gamified step trials, only the competition arm stayed above control after the intervention ended ([Patel et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6735420)).

Streaks are where loss aversion lives in habit apps, and they cut both ways. Seven experiments found that highlighting an intact streak raised later engagement, that people "just drop off once their streaks are broken", and that the penalty shrank when a streak could be repaired ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb)). Meanwhile habit formation took a median **66 days** (range 18 to 254) in Lally's study, and a single missed day did not materially change the curve ([The Behavioral Scientist](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit)), so the punitive reset is a retention device rather than a behaviour-change need. On motivation, expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice interest in lab tasks while **unexpected rewards showed no significant undermining** ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627); effect sizes in the notes are from memory and unverified), and a 183-study meta-analysis found incentives and intrinsic motivation "not necessarily antagonistic" ([Cerasoli et al. 2014](https://oa.mg/work/10.1037/a0035661)). That one finding matters most for design: a surprise that arrives unannounced is the least motivationally costly reward there is, and a chance reward the user expects every day is closer to a wage.

## German law ends where the stake ends, and EU pressure targets money

Germany regulates loot boxes through youth protection, not gambling law. Under § 3(1) GlüStV 2021 gambling requires that "für den Erwerb einer Gewinnchance ein Entgelt verlangt wird" ([gesetze-bayern.de](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/StVGlueStV2021-3)), and commentary is explicit that the stake element is met by real-money purchase, "nicht hingegen, wenn sie bloß erspielt wird"; in-game currency counts only if it was bought or can be resold ([beck-aktuell, April 2025](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/heute-im-recht/rechtspolitik-gesetzgebung/lootboxen-videospiele-gluecksspiel-verbraucherschutz-zufall-gewinnmoeglichkeit-2025-04-03)). Since 1 January 2023 the USK may weigh online risks such as purchase functions in its ratings ([mobiflip](https://www.mobiflip.de/shortnews/in-game-kaeufe-und-lootboxen-usk-erweitert-pruefkriterien/)); EA Sports FC moved from USK 0 to 12 for random paid items ([Tagesspiegel Background](https://background.tagesspiegel.de/digitalisierung-und-ki/briefing/was-taugt-das-deutsche-vorbild)), and by March 2026 a new criterion applied to about **30% of submissions**, raising the rating in about a third of those ([WinFuture](https://winfuture.de/news,157461.html)). On **21 November 2025** the Bundesrat asked the federal government to examine extending the JuSchG to loot boxes that fall outside § 3 GlüStV ([BR-Drs. 517/25(B)](https://dserver.bundestag.de/brd/2025/0517-25B.pdf)); the industry association game rejects the gambling comparison ([game, January 2026](https://www.game.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Stellungnahme-Entschliessung-BR-MV-JuSchG-Games-und-Lootboxen-akt.pdf)), and no bill has followed as of October 2026. The exact wording of § 10b JuSchG, which is believed to list "glücksspielähnliche Mechanismen" among interaction risks, could not be verified this session.

The EU has no loot-box law yet, but the pressure is rising and still aimed at money. The CPC Network's non-binding key principles of **21 March 2025** cover currencies bought with real money and explicitly exclude currencies earned only through gameplay ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/european-consumer-protection-network-issues-new-key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies-impact-for-gaming-and-gambling-entities-in-belgium-the-eu-and-beyond/)). On **30 September 2026** the network opened coordinated actions against nine companies including King, Mojang, Riot, Supercell and Ubisoft over loot boxes, misleading countdown timers, unfounded scarcity claims and direct exhortation of children ([Lewis Silkin, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/15/eu-consumer-protection-network-launches-action-involving-nine-video-games-compani-102o5do)). The Digital Fairness Act is provisionally scheduled for **11 November 2026** in a consumer package, with its content still unknown ([WKO](https://www.wko.at/information-consulting/werbung-marktkommunikation/aig-newsletter-5-october-2026)); the European Parliament has asked it to ban loot boxes and in-app currencies in games likely accessed by minors, and BEUC wants paid loot boxes banned outright ([BEUC](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/BEUC-X-2025-110_Towards_the_Digital_Fairness_Act.pdf)). The DSA Art. 28 guidelines of 14 July 2025 name virtual currencies and loot boxes as exploitative practices toward children ([A&L Goodbody](https://www.algoodbody.com/insights-publications/protection-of-minors-on-online-platforms-european-commission-adopts-article-281-dsa-guidelines)), but they bind online platforms that host user content, which a single-purpose posture page is not.

Elsewhere, the trend is age gates on paid chance and odds disclosure. PEGI, from June 2026, rates games selling paid random items at **at least PEGI 16**, time-limited offers at 12, play-by-appointment that punishes absence at 12 and play-by-appointment that only rewards return at **7** ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/pegi-launches-interactive-risk-categories-overhauls-age-ratings-for-loot-boxes-in-game-spending-and-communication-features/)). Austria's Supreme Court held on 18 December 2025 that FIFA packs are not gambling ([esportslegal.news](https://esportslegal.news/2026/01/28/austrian-supreme-court-loot-boxes/)); the Netherlands' Raad van State reached the same result in 2022; Belgium's 2018 ban stands but is largely unenforced, with 82 of the top 100 grossing iPhone games still carrying loot boxes ([QMUL](https://qmul.ac.uk/law/news/2022/items/study-claims-country-wide-loot-box-ban-isnt-practically-achievable.html)). Brazil's ECA Digital bars paid loot boxes in games likely accessed by minors from 17 March 2026 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/brazil-bans-loot-boxes-for-under-18s-in-online-child-safety-measure)); Spain's pending minors bill defines loot boxes as activated with "real money or a virtual object" ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/es/insights/2025/spain/artículo-patricia-peregrín-y-jaime-delgado)), which could reach a box opened with earned coins if read literally. The one rule found that bites with no money at all is Australia's: since 22 September 2024 **simulated gambling earns R18+** regardless of real money, while casino imagery without interactive gambling is not restricted ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/australia-sets-new-rules-for-video-games-with-gambling-like-content)).

For deshrimp the legal position is comfortable and depends on one invariant. A free random bonus with no purchase path, no purchasable currency and no cash-out fails the stake test in every gambling regime found, falls outside the CPC principles by their own scope, and triggers none of the paid-random-item rating rules; USK and PEGI rate games, not websites, in any case. What would change that is a single purchase path, a donation that buys coins, a coin that can be traded, or slot and roulette imagery. No regulator guidance was found on free, non-monetised chance rewards in non-game apps, and the DFA text is not yet public, so this should be rechecked when it appears in November.

## Critics draw the line at whose goal the mechanic serves

The critiques agree on a test more than on a list. Zagal, Björk and Lewis define dark game patterns as features used deliberately against the player's interest and group them as temporal (grinding, playing by appointment), monetary and social ([Deceptive Design summary](https://www.deceptive.design/articles/dark-patterns-in-the-design-of-games)); King and Delfabbro call schemes that hide the long-term cost of play until the player is invested "predatory monetization" ([Flinders University](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/predatory-monetization-schemes-in-video-games-eg-loot-boxes-and-i/)); the Norwegian Consumer Council's "Insert Coin" report, backed by more than 20 consumer groups in 18 countries, focuses on exploited biases, layered currencies and minors ([Forbrukerrådet](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/siste-nytt/loot-boxes-how-the-gaming-industry-manipulates-and-exploits-consumers/)). A free app sidesteps the monetary category, but the temporal patterns do not need money. Natasha Dow Schüll's four features of the slot "machine zone", **solitude, bottomlessness ("There's no natural stopping point"), speed and teasing**, are a ready checklist for any app ([NPR via WUNC, April 2026](https://www.wunc.org/2026-04-21/the-surprising-origin-of-4-features-that-superglue-kids-and-adults-to-screens)). Nir Eyal's defence of the "variable reward" step in *Hooked* rests on asking whether the product materially improves the user's life ([WDO](https://wdo.org/?p=141600)), which is at least the right question for a health tool.

Designers who use randomness honestly tame it. Warcraft III and Dota 2 use a pseudo-random distribution in which each failure raises the next chance and a success resets it, so a nominal 25% proc starts at about 8.5% and effects "rarely proc many times in a row, or go a long time without happening" ([Liquipedia](https://liquipedia.net/dota2/Random_Number_Generation)). Pokemon GO's Adventure Sync pays for real kilometres walked, counted with the game closed, as "all guaranteed reward bundles plus one of the random reward bundles" ([Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net:443/wiki/Adventure_Sync)). Mainstream wellbeing apps have moved toward forgiveness: Apple added Pause Rings in watchOS 11 after years of complaints that its streaks assumed rings closed every day, even when sick ([Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/?p=871055)); Headspace lets users hide streaks ([Built for Mars](https://builtformars.com/ux-bites/hiding-your-headspace-streaks)); in Finch a missed day means the bird "stays home", with no punishment ([Headway](https://makeheadway.com/blog/finch-app-review/)). On the other side, Duolingo's paid streak freeze has been criticised as charging for the anxiety the streak creates ([Desirability Lab](https://desirabilitylab.com/backfill/backfill-2023-154-duolingo-streak-design)), and "You made Duo sad" as "weaponizing guilt" ([The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification)).

Deshrimp has a tension most of these apps lack, and the earlier report already named it: Shrimpcoin is earned per good reading, so every minute in the chair pays and nothing pays for getting up, while WHO's 2020 guidelines recommend breaking up long sitting ([PMC7691115](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7691115)). Since his decision rules out break rewards, the one lever left for chance is **where it attaches**. A surprise that grows with run length, or a timed event that pulls the user back to the screen, would deepen the problem; a surprise attached to visiting, or a fixed-rate bonus that does not grow with bout length, leaves the incentive shape as it is.

## What this means for deshrimp

Deshrimp already uses chance in the right way once. The fart is twelve recordings dealt from a shuffled deck so that none repeats before all have played ([`CHANGELOG.md`](../CHANGELOG.md), 2026-10-05), which is a shuffle bag: random in order, guaranteed in total, with no droughts and nothing to chase. Every recommendation below reuses that pattern rather than a free-running random number, because a deck gives the PRD property (no long dry spells, no "maybe the next one") and makes the odds trivially honest: "1 in every 30" is a fact, not an average.

The current income for reference: at the default one reading a second, a perfect run pays **12 coin a minute**, 6 from "10x sitting straight" and 6 from "10 in a row", so a streak bonus fires about every 10 seconds; each visited day after the first adds 20.

### Worth trying, ranked

**1. A surprise top-up on the daily bonus (serves "öfter").** Keep the guaranteed 20 and add one card from a seven-card bag on each paid day: {0, 0, 0, 5, 5, 10, 30}, so a top-up averages about 7 coin, the 30 is guaranteed once per seven paid days, and the bag reshuffles after seven draws. A missed day draws no card, so nothing is lost and the 30 still comes; that keeps it on the "rewards return" side of PEGI's line rather than "punishes absence". Add beginner's luck in the Habitica sense by making the device's first draw, on its second day, never a zero, so the first return visit is the one that feels good. Show it as its own snackbar line in the existing format, for example "+10 · surprise", with no wheel, no spin animation and no "come back tomorrow" copy. This ranks first because it pays for visiting rather than for sitting, so it adds no pressure to stay in the chair. Its cost is that it strengthens a daily appointment that already exists; that is acceptable only while missing a day costs nothing.

**2. A "lucky shrimp" on streak bonuses (serves "beim Kennenlernen").** Deal streak bonuses from a deck of 30 in which one card pays **5 instead of 1**. At a perfect pace that is one lucky shrimp per 5 minutes of straight sitting, close to the golden cookie's 447-second mean, and it raises income by 0.8 coin a minute, from 12 to 12.8 (+7%). The first unlock moves from about 4.2 to about 3.9 minutes, which keeps his "nach 4 minuten ca" call, and the full 7,200-coin ladder from 10 hours to about 9.4. On the device's first day, use a first deck of 12 so the first lucky shrimp lands within the first 2 minutes of good sitting, which is the onboarding moment the gamification is for. It is credited automatically: no click, no expiry, no timer, so missing the snackbar costs nothing. Because the deck advances one card per streak bonus, lucky shrimps grow exactly in proportion to sitting, never faster with a longer bout. It ranks second because it does still pay for sitting, and because the evidence says it will add delight rather than retention.

**3. Unannounced milestone gifts.** One-off gifts that the user does not know are coming, at counts spaced dense early and sparse late in the style of Cookie Clicker's 1, 7, 27, 77: for example **+25 on the 7th visited day and +50 on the 27th and 77th**, or at 1,000 and 10,000 good readings. These are the reward type with the best motivational evidence (unexpected rewards did not undermine interest in the 1999 meta-analysis), they cannot be farmed because they happen once, and counting visited days rather than consecutive days means they never punish a gap. Strictly they are surprise rather than chance, which is the point: they deliver the novelty of a random reward without a schedule the user learns to expect. Never list them in advance, or they become goals with a deadline-like pull.

**4. Keep and extend the shuffle bag for sounds.** Any future sound that ships as several recordings should use the same no-repeat deck. This is chance used for variety in the alarm, which resists habituation to the signal, not for reward, and it carries none of the risks below.

| Mechanic | Parameters | Effect on income | Pays for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daily surprise top-up | 20 fixed + bag {0,0,0,5,5,10,30}; first draw never 0 | ~+7 per visited day | Visiting |
| Lucky shrimp | 1 in 30 streak bonuses pays 5; first-day deck of 12 | +0.8/min (+7%) | Sitting, linearly |

### Avoid, and why

**Clickable timed events** (golden-cookie style, an item that must be tapped within seconds): they summon attention to a page whose job is to sit in the background, and they turn the tool into something to watch. **Mystery boxes or gacha bought with Shrimpcoin** (a random sound for 300 coin, a pity counter towards a rare one): this is the loot-box structure itself, it replaces the user's choice of alarm with chance, it is the one design that Spain's draft wording could reach, and it would put a purchase-shaped habit in an app that students and teenagers may well use. **Visible pity counters or "next lucky in N"** displays: they convert a surprise into a countdown to chase. **Near-miss displays**: when a streak breaks at 9 of 10, the page should simply restart the count rather than show "so close" or "lost at 9"; near-misses raise the urge to continue, and highlighting broken runs lowers later engagement. **Losses disguised as wins and reward sounds**: never celebrate a zero card or a tiny payout as a win, and never give a payout a sound, both because sound inflates remembered wins and because sound in deshrimp means "sit up" and must stay unambiguous. **Day streaks, streak insurance and wagers**: a consecutive-day streak would make the daily bonus a loss device, insurance against it charges for anxiety the app created, and a Duolingo-style pledge, though it is the best-evidenced retention mechanic here (+14% D7), runs on loss framing and would make coins at stake hinge on attendance; if it is ever tried, it should be opt-in and counted as days visited out of seven, never as consecutive days. **Coin decay, expiry or "use it or lose it"**: sunk-cost pressure with no benefit to posture. **Multipliers that rise with run length**, random or not: they pay most for the longest unbroken sitting, which is the one thing not to pay more for. **Wheels, reels, roulette and casino imagery**: harmless legally in Germany for a free page, but simulated gambling is exactly what Australia rates R18+, and the look invites the comparison the rest of the design avoids.

### Guardrails

The invariant that keeps all of this outside gambling and loot-box law is that **Shrimpcoin can never be bought, sold, traded or cashed out**, directly or through donations; it belongs in `CHANGELOG.md` as a recorded decision so a later change has to overturn it explicitly. Chance only ever adds on top of a fixed payout and never takes anything away, so there is no losing outcome to disguise. Every chance mechanic uses a deck, not a free-running random number, and its odds are stated in plain words where the coin is explained ("one streak bonus in 30 pays 5"), in all thirteen languages. Chance should stay a small share of income, on the order of 10% or less; at the numbers above it is about 7% of sitting income plus a third more on the daily bonus. Nothing is time-limited, nothing requires a click, nothing sends a notification, and no copy says "come back tomorrow". Chance attaches to visits or to fixed-rate events, never to bout length. The visuals follow `src/ui/DESIGN.md` (accent as a budget, no new animation without a reason) and respect reduced motion, and snackbar text keeps saying what the coins are for, as he asked. Finally, deshrimp keeps everything on the device and has no analytics, so it cannot A/B test any of this; the honest way to judge it is to ship one mechanic at a time, ask a few users, and drop what nobody notices.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what chance is for. In the products that use it most, randomness is the lure and loss is the hook: pity counters, expiry windows and streaks are what make stopping feel costly, and the measured harms follow money, not dice. Strip out the loss and the money and what remains is a small, well-evidenced pleasure, the unexpected gift, whose motivational record is better than the promised reward's precisely because nobody planned around it. That is the only part of the toolkit a posture trainer needs.

For deshrimp the open question is not legal, since a coin that cannot be bought is not gambling anywhere examined, but structural: the coin pays for sitting and nothing pays for standing up. Chance cannot fix that, but it can avoid worsening it, which is why the best-ranked mechanic here pays for showing up rather than for staying. If the camera ever learns to tell a real break from an empty frame, the strongest use of a surprise reward would be to put it there.
