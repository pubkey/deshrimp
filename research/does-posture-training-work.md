# Does any of this work?

A page that beeps at you for sitting badly rests on two claims: that bad sitting
matters, and that being told about it changes anything. Both are weaker than the
posture industry pretends. This page is what the literature actually says, so
that the copy on the page never claims more than it can carry. Checked
**9 September 2026** against PubMed.

The short version: **the mechanism has support, the endpoint does not.** "Notice
when you have been in one position too long" is defensible. "Fix your posture" and
"reduce your back pain" are not.

## Does forward head posture even relate to neck pain?

[Mahmoud 2019, *Curr Rev Musculoskelet Med*, PMID 31773477](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31773477/) —
systematic review and meta-analysis, 15 cross-sectional studies:

> Ten studies compared FHP between a group of asymptomatic participants and a
> group of participants with neck pain and an overall **mean difference of 4.84
> (95% CI = 0.14, 9.54)**, indicating a significant between-group difference,
> **contrary to adolescent (MD = -1.05; 95% CI = -4.23, 2.12)**. Eight studies
> showed significant negative correlations between FHP and neck pain intensity
> (**r = -0.55**)… **No association was found between FHP and most of neck pain
> measures in adolescents.**

Read the caveats: **all fifteen studies were cross-sectional**, so there is no
direction of causation; the adult confidence interval's lower bound is 0.14°,
which barely excludes zero; the effect vanishes entirely in adolescents; and the
magnitude is comparable to the measurement error of the gold-standard method (see
[`what-a-front-webcam-can-measure.md`](./what-a-front-webcam-can-measure.md)).
It is an association in adults, of uncertain direction, of marginal size.

## Does posture cause back pain? Increasingly, the answer is no

| Source | What it found |
| --- | --- |
| [Swain 2020, *J Biomech*, PMID 31451200](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31451200/) — umbrella review of 41 systematic reviews, appraised against the Bradford Hill criteria | "**Despite the availability of many reviews, there is no consensus regarding causality of physical exposure to LBP. Association has been documented but does not provide a causal explanation for LBP.**" Reviews restricted to *prospective* studies were **less** able to reach consistent conclusions. |
| [Slater 2019, *JOSPT*, PMID 31366294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31366294/) — "'Sit Up Straight': Time to Re-evaluate" | "**Despite the absence of strong evidence to support these common beliefs, a large posture industry has flourished**, with many interventions and products claiming to 'correct' posture and prevent pain." |
| [Driessen 2010, PMID 20360197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20360197/) | Ergonomic interventions for workers **have not reduced** work-related back pain. |
| [Saraceni 2020, PMID 31775556](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31775556/) and [2021, PMID 34288926](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34288926/) | No evidence that round-back lifting predicts back pain. Workers **without** back pain were **more likely** to lift with a stooped posture. |
| [O'Sullivan, Straker & Saraceni, *The Conversation* 2022](https://theconversation.com/having-good-posture-doesnt-prevent-back-pain-and-bad-posture-doesnt-cause-it-183732) | "**No single posture protects a person from back pain**"; "**learning to vary and adopt different postures that are comfortable is likely to be more helpful than rigidly adhering to a specific 'good' posture**." |
| Beth Linker, *Slouch: Posture Panic in Modern America* (Princeton UP, 2024), [summarised in *Psyche*](https://psyche.co/ideas/in-defence-of-slouching-the-bad-science-behind-good-posture) | "**Negligible evidence to support a causal link between slouching and back pain in an otherwise healthy person.**" Cites an Australian study finding a slight upper-back slump may be *protective* of neck pain versus upright posture in young women. Traces the whole ideology to the American Posture League of 1914 and its eugenic uses. |

There is a counterweight, and it should be recorded fairly:
[Guduru 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8959976/) reports
improper sitting as a neck-pain risk factor at **OR 1.97 (1.39–2.78)**, and
workplace sitting at **OR 1.73 (1.46–2.03)** for neck and shoulder pain. But the
same reviews call the **prospective** evidence "inconclusive" and the
cross-sectional findings "inconsistent". The honest reading is that the
associations are real and the causal claim is not established.

## The one claim that holds up: duration, not shape

[Christensen 2023, *Appl Ergon*, PMID 36958253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36958253/) —
randomized crossover, 25 healthy participants, four 15-minute typing tasks
(upright or slumped, with or without forearm support), pain rated every minute,
EMG from trapezius, serratus anterior and deltoids:

> **All tasks caused clinically relevant increased NRS (>= 2/10) compared to
> baseline (P < 0.001).** NRS was higher in Task-D [slumped, unsupported] and
> lower in Task-B [upright, unsupported] than others… **CONCLUSION: A 15-min task
> caused pain irrespective of posture with some causing larger changes than
> others.**

This is the single most directly relevant experimental study found, and it
supports **both halves** of the framing already in the README: posture modulates
the magnitude (slumped and unsupported was measurably worst, with higher
trapezius EMG), and **duration alone produced clinically relevant pain in every
condition, including perfect upright posture**.

Supporting it:
[Sawaf 2026, *Cureus*, PMID 42621015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42621015/) —
"**Static posture alone does not consistently predict chronic neck pain.**
**Sustained or repeated** cervicothoracic loading may nevertheless increase
muscular demand… **when exposure exceeds tissue tolerance and recovery
capacity.** However, direct… evidence in patients with chronic neck pain remains
limited."
And [Saiklang 2021, PMID 32799753](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32799753/),
which found spinal stature loss over 41 minutes of sitting was measurably
recovered (3.29 mm) by periodic deep-trunk activation — periodic interruption of
sustained load has a measurable mechanical effect.

**So "sustained deviation, not a single moment, is the risk" is not a hedge. It
is the best-supported claim available**, it is the one thing the revisionists and
the traditionalists agree on, and it happens to be exactly what the loudest
critics on Hacker News say back at posture apps (see
[`open-source-and-hacker-news.md`](./open-source-and-hacker-news.md)). It should
be the product thesis, stated early.

## Does biofeedback change anything? The nearest RCT is null

| Study | Design | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [**Lindegård 2024**, *Appl Ergon*, PMID 37883913](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37883913/) | RCT, n=39 young adult computer users with neck and upper-extremity symptoms, biofeedback + ergonomic information, 4 sessions over 3 months, followed up at 6 and 12 months | **Null.** "**The intervention did not significantly improve working technique and working postures, nor reduce pain intensity and perceived exertion in the intervention group compared to the control group.**" There *was* a significant pain reduction **across the whole group including controls** — a strong regression-to-the-mean signal — and a non-significant trend toward fewer unbroken computer hours. |
| [Szeto 2026, PMID 42429914](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42429914/) | 3-arm RCT, device worn **2 hours daily for 6 weeks**, control did home stretching | Positive but weakly reported: both biofeedback groups improved on pain and NDI **in pre-post within-group comparisons**, not in a between-group interaction. Within-group pre-post significance is a well-known weak form of evidence. |
| [Immediate effects of real-time postural biofeedback, *Gait Posture*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0966636218317223) | immediate-effect study | Short-term postural and EMG change demonstrated. Says nothing about durability. |
| [Active break and postural shift cluster-RCT, n=193, 6-month follow-up](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8091075/) | The most rigorous design in the set | Notably it intervenes on **breaks and postural shifts** — variation over time — not on holding one correct posture. |

**Lindegård 2024 is the closest published analogue to what this page does, and it
failed its primary endpoints.** The positive studies use dedicated hardware,
supervised protocols and two hours a day for six weeks.

**Copy consequence: make no pain claims.** They are unsupported and easy for a
critic to falsify with one citation.

## Will people just tune it out?

Yes, and the literature on that is unusually good, because computer security
spent a decade studying it.

| Source | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| [Vance 2018, *MIS Quarterly*](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.25300/MISQ/2018/14124) — fMRI, eye tracking and field experiments | Habituation to warnings is not laziness. It is measurable **neural repetition suppression** developing over repeated exposures. |
| [**Anderson 2015, CHI**](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2702123.2702322) — the most actionable result here | fMRI showed "a **dramatic drop in the visual processing centers of the brain after only the second exposure** to a warning". A **polymorphic** warning — one that repeatedly changes appearance — "was substantially more resistant to habituation" and **held that advantage across a five-day longitudinal test**. |
| [Vance 2019 SOUPS / 2025 *MISQ*, "The Fog of Warnings"](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2019/presentation/vance) | Habituation to a *frequent, unimportant* notification **transfers** to a *novel, important* warning. But "**changing the visual appearance of the warning to make it less similar to the notification made generalization substantially less likely.**" |
| Escalating-alarm design, [alarm-fatigue literature](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/66118) | An escalating alert should "progressively increas[e] the amplitude over time, ideally **starting at a level barely detectable and increasing to a level that cannot be ignored**", escalated by time in the unacknowledged state. |

### What that says about the design we already have

| Element | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **The ramping alarm** (quarter volume to full over four consecutive bad readings) | **Well aligned.** This is textbook escalation-by-time-unacknowledged, and matches the "barely detectable → impossible to ignore" pattern almost exactly. |
| **Five recorded sounds** | **Better than it probably realises.** This is a *polymorphic* alert, and Anderson 2015 is direct experimental evidence that varying the stimulus preserves attention across days where a fixed one collapses after about two exposures. **Worth doing: rotate or randomise the five rather than treating them as a fixed user preference, and consider more than five.** |
| **Firing on every single crooked reading, at one per second** | **The biggest habituation risk in the design.** Second-by-second repetition of an identical stimulus is the textbook habituation regime. The mitigation is already implicit in the sustained-deviation model: alert on a *sustained* breach, with a minimum interval between alarms and a refractory period, rather than per frame. |
| **The favicon going green/yellow/red** | **The right ambient channel** — glanceable, non-interruptive, and it does not spend the attention budget the alarm needs. But note the Fog of Warnings result: a constant low-stakes visual indicator can **transfer** habituation onto the higher-stakes alarm if the two look and feel similar. Keep the two channels perceptually distinct. |

## What this means for the copy

1. **Lead with duration, not with "good posture".** It is the best-supported
   claim, and it disarms the strongest objection.
2. **Never claim pain reduction.** Lindegård 2024 is a clean null on almost
   exactly this intervention.
3. **Never claim a CVA or an accuracy percentage.** See the sibling page.
4. **Keep saying the thresholds are set, not established.** They are: no
   literature gives a degree limit for lateral trunk or shoulder tilt at all.
5. The honest sentence the whole folder supports is something like: *sitting in
   one position for a long time is the part with evidence behind it; this page
   notices when you have been, and makes a noise.*
