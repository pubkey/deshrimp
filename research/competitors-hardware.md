# Hardware — the incumbents, and the graveyard behind them

Posture wearables are what people picture when they hear "posture trainer", they
had all the money, and almost all of them are dead. This page is the evidence for
that, because it is the strongest single argument a free web page has. Checked
**9 September 2026** against vendor sites, Amazon, Trustpilot, the App Store and
Google Play, Kickstarter mirrors, the Wayback Machine and PubMed.

## Five things worth knowing before the tables

1. **The category leader is a zombie with a live storefront.** Upright
   Technologies was acquired by DarioHealth for $31M in January 2021 and pivoted
   to employer-channel digital MSK. The device still sells, but its **Trustpilot
   score is 1.4/5 with 83% one-star reviews**, its **Android app is 2.3/5**, and
   2026 reviews describe orders that never shipped and support that never
   answered.
2. **Every serious VC-funded competitor in this segment is dead.** Lumo Bodytech
   (assignment for benefit of creditors, 29 June 2018), ALEX/NAMU, Zikto, Darma,
   Opter, Prana. Wearable X still sells $99 sensor yoga pants against **an iOS app
   last updated on 1 February 2018**.
3. **The mass market is a $9-$40 elastic strap, not a $79 gadget.** Amazon's
   posture-corrector shelf is 3.6-4.1-star braces at $9-$40. The Upright GO 2 sits
   at **3.8 stars, 2 792 ratings, $79.80**, ranked #4 886 in Sports & Outdoors.
4. **The failure modes are measured, not anecdotal.** Amazon's own aspect
   sentiment on the GO 2: reliability **25 positive / 136 negative**; value **91 /
   151**; adhesion **70 / 82**. The 2019 *Sensors* review of commercial postural
   devices found **half of users stopped using their device, a third within six
   months**.
5. **The market-size reports are worthless.** 2025/2026 estimates for the same
   market range from **$1.2bn to $2.51bn** with CAGRs from 5.7% to 18%.

**The instructive one is Lumo.** It raised $15M+, shipped two well-reviewed
products, and produced **the only real validation data in the entire segment** —
LumoBack was test-retest reliable and differentiated low-back-pain patients. It
still ran out of money in about seven years. The best-validated product in this
category is the dead one.

> "It would be a good product if it just tap you once when your posture is not

> "**Seems like the device requires an over-correction** to maintain proper

**Watch Kodgem, not Upright.** Upright is a legacy brand being harvested; Kodgem

The volume is at $9-$40, and **the category's best seller has 5 127 ratings** —
the whole shelf is small. Upright charges five times as much for the same 3.8
stars as a $16 strap. There is no premium tier this market has validated.

**And the clinicians are uniformly negative on the whole category**
([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/health/health-wellness/are-posture-correctors-useful-a1211785884/)):

> "Generally, we think of those as gimmicks" — Scott Beadnell, DPT, Oregon Health
> & Science University, who has never recommended one in thirteen years.

> "The danger is that people would maybe develop dependence on it, and then
> actually that might lead to worsening of the weakness." — Eugene Wai, MD, spine
> surgeon.

CR's bottom line — *"there's no strong evidence that bad posture causes chronic
back pain"* — **undercuts this entire category, us included.** See
[`does-posture-training-work.md`](./does-posture-training-work.md). Position on
awareness and habit, never on pain.

Worth noting the counter-example: **BetterBack** is a **passive strap with no
sensors, no app and no battery**, and reportedly does ~$5M a year, unfunded. A
dumb $60 strap outsold most of the smart devices on this page.

## Chairs and textiles, briefly

**Chairs.** Herman Miller tried sensing with Live OS and shelved it; the
surveillance framing is why ([Quartz](https://qz.com/work/1218346/herman-millers-new-aeron-chair-is-an-office-spy-collecting-data-on-your-every-move)).
Embody, Autonomous, Branch and Ergotron all ship pure mechanical furniture with no
posture sensing at all. Chair sensors also **cannot see the head or neck**, which
is the dominant screen-work problem.

**Textiles.** Hexoskin is alive and clinically validated but **has no posture
metric** — it does ECG, HRV and respiration, so it is adjacent rather than
competing. AlignMed and Forme are passive compression garments, exactly the class
a 2019 *Scandinavian Journal of Pain* review criticised for the fact that **none
of the six posture-shirt studies enrolled anyone with actual pain** — *"That's a
major flaw in itself."*

## The seven structural weaknesses

| | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **You have to buy it, then keep buying** | $79-$149 up front, then $9.95 adhesive packs, plus customs for anyone outside the US: *"ho dovuto pagare una tassa per lo sdoganamento di 38$"*. The *Sensors* review names cost as an access barrier that excludes *"lower socioeconomic populations — those who need interventions most."* |
| **You have to charge it, and it dies** | Amazon reliability **25 positive / 136 negative**. *"It comes with a special USB-C to USB-C cable as a regular USB-C cable does not to work."* *"The item turns off on its own being fully charged, and randomly."* |
| **You have to remember to wear it, and you won't** | The segment's own literature: **"Half reported having stopped using their device, with one third doing so before 6 months"**, and *"there is typically no external reminder to wear such wearables"*. **Our cue is the thing you are already doing: opening a tab.** |
| **The adhesive fails** | Amazon adhesion **70 positive / 82 negative** — the only aspect where negatives win on a premium product. Vendor says reusable ten times; reviewers say three days; one improvises with hardware-store tape; another lost the device off their back. |
| **It is one tilt sensor pretending to be posture analysis** | The GO S has **one** sensor. It cannot distinguish forward head posture from a whole-torso lean, cannot see shoulder tilt, cannot see head tilt. Hence *"it buzzes at the slightest movement. You can't look down or sideways"*. **Three independent angles from a full skeletal pose is strictly more information than an inclinometer taped to one vertebra.** |
| **It is not autonomous — it needs your phone, an account and a cloud** | *"it constantly (!) needs to be connected to my phone"*; frequent BLE disconnects; account, email and reportedly a phone number required; and a privacy policy that names **insurance companies**. |
| **The vendor can die, and your hardware dies with it** | See the graveyard. A dead vendor bricks a Bluetooth device that needs an app that needs a login that needs a server. **A static MIT page with local storage cannot be bricked by anyone, including its author.** |

## Where hardware genuinely wins

Be honest; some of these cannot be closed.

| Their advantage | Why it is real | Can we answer it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Works away from the desk** | Walking, standing, meetings, commuting. Reviewers use it snowshoeing. | **No.** Concede it and own desk work, which is where the problem is anyway. |
| **Measures the thoracic spine directly** | An IMU between the shoulder blades reads that segment's actual sagittal inclination. A single 2D webcam infers depth from a monocular projection, and the literature is explicit that single-view markerless systems are *"fundamentally limited by depth ambiguity and occlusion."* | **Partly.** Head-forward is our most depth-sensitive angle; shoulder tilt and head tilt are in-plane and robust. See [`what-a-front-webcam-can-measure.md`](./what-a-front-webcam-can-measure.md). Worth remembering that **nobody in this category has demonstrated accuracy** — the leader's evidence is 26 students with no objective posture measurement at all. |
| **No camera required** | Some users, and many IT policies, will not run a webcam during work. A camera invites a surveillance reflex even when nothing leaves the machine — it is what killed Herman Miller's sensor chair. | **Partly.** "No frame leaves the device, no server exists, MIT source you can read" is a strong answer, but it must be the *first* thing on the page, because the objection is emotional and fires before anyone reads a privacy note. |
| **Works with the screen off or eyes elsewhere** | Paper, phone calls, a second monitor outside the frame. | **No.** Real limitation. |
| **Passive once attached** | Stick it on and forget. | **Partly** — a pinned tab plus the green/yellow/red favicon is a decent analogue, and that ambient indicator is a genuinely good answer here. |
| **Regulatory framing** | Upright is FDA-registered — a low bar, but a marketing asset. | **No, and do not try.** Stay out of medical claims entirely. |

**Base values differ by 2.1x for the same year. CAGRs differ by 3.2x.** None
publish a methodology or define what counts as a posture corrector.

Direct evidence they are unreliable: Market Research Future's "key players" list
includes *"Posture Corrector Inc"*, *"Sitting Solutions"* and *"Sitting Well"* —
generic phrases, not companies. Coherent's list includes **Thermo Fisher
Scientific** and **Abbott**, neither of which sells a posture corrector.

A reality check against the actual numbers on this page: the category's Amazon
best seller has 5 127 ratings, the leader's flagship listing has 2 792, its app
has 100K+ lifetime Android downloads, and its parent company's *entire*
multi-condition digital-health business does $5.2M a quarter and is shrinking.
**A "$1.5-2.5bn market" is not visible anywhere in those numbers.** Whatever the
reports are counting is overwhelmingly $15 elastic straps sold through
pharmacies.

If a figure is ever needed, cite Grand View as the most conservative and say
explicitly that vendors disagree by a factor of two. Never plan against one.

## What to take from this

1. **Lead with vendor mortality, not price.** Price is easy to match; twelve dead
   vendors in a table is not. *"Every posture wearable you can name is
   discontinued, and the one that isn't scores 1.4 on Trustpilot"* is a sharper
   hook than *"free"*.
2. **The privacy contrast is unusually favourable because the leader's own policy
   is unusually bad.** They collect diagnoses, medications and blood tests, ship
   to Facebook and Google, and declare cross-app tracking identifiers. We send
   nothing anywhere. That is checkable and specific, not a vibe.
3. **Twelve languages against one.** The market leader's app is English-only and
   its own German customers say so in its own review widget.
4. **Three angles against one inclinometer.** The recurring hardware complaint is
   false positives from a sensor that cannot tell "looked down at my keyboard"
   from "slouched". *"It's a gyroscope in a box"* is their customer's phrase, not
   ours.
5. **Concede mobility, own the desk.**

## Not verified

- **Reddit is missing** — blocked at both fetch and search level for the research
  tooling. Amazon, Trustpilot, Google Play and vendor-widget verbatims were
  substituted, which are arguably stronger evidence anyway.
- **Whether Upright charges an app subscription is unproven.** A verified reviewer
  asserts it; there is no Play IAP badge and no published price. A likely confound
  is an unrelated app called "UpRight - Posture Coach" that charges $24.99/yr.
  Treat it as a live *perception* among buyers rather than a fact.
- **Herman Miller Live OS has no formal discontinuation announcement**; the
  conclusion is inferred from product absence plus press.
- **Athos's current trading status is conflicting.** Consumer cloud and firmware
  reportedly ended in Q2 2022, but the domain still responds.
- Fyt-02 (VRGO, launched May 2026), Enyware and Duorest On: crowdfunding outcomes
  could not be confirmed.
- "Berkelium" and "ArkiHealth" were searched for and **do not exist** as posture
  products. "Arki" is Zikto's own successor brand.
